Page Sections

Full-width sections a page is built from, from the hero at the top to the link bank at the bottom. All from CMU's standard template; nothing here needs the kit.

Page Sections

A page can be a run of full-width sections, one after another: a .content block, a grid, a section bar, another grid. On a real page they go straight inside #content, not inside one another; the examples below sit in boxes only so the page can show them. In Cascade, each is a content section of its own (WYSIWYG, Grid, or Wide). 

<main id="content">
  <div class="breadcrumbs"> … </div>
  <div class="content"> … </div>
  <div class="grid column2 red invert"> … </div>
  <div class="grid column2 grey boxes"> … </div>
  <div class="content wide red invert globe"> … </div>
  <div class="grid column2"> … </div>
</main>

Each section's heading is an <h1>, as on CMU's Cascade pages, and headings inside a section (a card, a sub-topic) are <h2> and below. That holds for every section, including the first one after a hero: the hero's tagline is an <h1> too, and stays the biggest heading on the page. Pick levels by what a heading contains, never by where it falls on the page; screen reader users skim a page by its heading levels.

Section Headings With a Line

An optional thin line over a section's heading, to mark where one topic ends and the next begins on a long page, as on Computing Services' video production page. Add the template's rule-top to the heading. The line sits right on top of the heading; the section's own padding gives the space above it. On a color band (invert) the line turns white. Its partner, rule-bottom, puts the line under a heading instead, as in the sidebar.

Hero

A full-width photo at the top of a page, before the breadcrumbs, as on First-Year Orientation's pages. It's a div.hero with the photo as its background: a 900 × 600 version in style for phones, and a 1400 × 700 version in data-img, which the template's script swaps in on wider screens. Give it role="img" and an aria-label describing the photo. In Cascade, it's the page's Hero.

Over the photo, either a line of text in a dark band (div.text, with an <h1> and an optional <h2>), or a white box that links somewhere (a.box, with left or right), as on University Health Services' home page.

I Want To…

The same grid without a color: white boxes with a thin border on the page's own background, headed by what people come to do, as on CMU's ID+ page. Here each box's title is the link, in the template's red, instead of a cta under the text. Use linked titles when the title says it all, and a cta when the action needs its own words. Phrase the titles as the end of the heading's sentence: "I want to… Borrow a Camera".

Carousel

Feature sections that slide, with arrows at the sides and a dot for each slide, as on CMU's Visit page. Put two or more grid column2 slides in a js-flickityCustom, and load the template's carousel script after its main one (Cascade adds it for you): 

<script src="https://www.cmu.edu/common/standard-v6/js/flickity.pkgd.min.js"></script>

It never moves by itself. Turn off the dots or arrows with data-navDotsVisible="false" or data-navArrowsVisible="false", and stop it going round from the last slide to the first with data-wrapAround="false". A color on the wrapper colors every slide. On phones the arrows sit over the slides' headings; turn them off there if your headings are long, since people can swipe instead.

Use it sparingly: people rarely look past the first slide, so put the most important one first, and never hide something people need in a later slide.

Timeline Cards

The steps of a process in date order, a card for each with a small icon, the step's name, and its dates, on a dark band, as on Human Resources' performance management page. It's a grid column4 darkgreen boxes (or column3) with an <h1>. Each step's name can link to its section further down the page.

  • The icon is a small image with the template's floatright, before the name. Give it empty alt text: the name says it all.
  • The dates go under a rule, bold and centered with aligncenter. Write ranges with an en dash: May 4–July 10.
  • Keep to the same years in each card, or show the year on every date, so the order is clear at a glance.

grid column4 darkgreen boxes, floatright, aligncenter

Photo Grid

Linked photos with a title over each, as on the HUB's Academic Calendar (three across) and Family Programs' Parents page (four across). The whole photo is the link. Use column2, column3, or column4; an <h1> first in the grid is its heading, across the full width. Only an <h1> spans the grid: a paragraph inside it becomes one of the cells, so put any sentence about the photos in their titles instead. Use photos that are all the same shape (the HUB's are 3:2, such as 900 × 600), or the rows come out ragged and the titles don't line up. At four across, keep titles to two or three words, or they wrap; a sentence under the title fits at two or three across. On phones the photos stack.

Announcements

A full-width band across the top of a page, right under the breadcrumbs, for something everyone who comes to the page should know first: who the page is for, a deadline, a change. Site authors write it and take it down; it says the same thing to everyone. It's a content wide block in a template color. Keep to one at a time, and a sentence or two.

  • Dark gray (darkgrey invert) for who a page is for, with the text in bold, as on Stellic's page for staff and faculty.
  • Gold for a time-sensitive update, with a bold lead-in, as on CMU's Visit page. Gold takes dark text, so it needs no invert. When the whole notice is one short statement, make it a centered <h3> instead, as on the Tartan Bike Project's page; center it with the template's aligncenter, not an inline style.
  • Blue (blue invert) for a note set apart lower on the page, with an <h4> for small bold capitals, as on the HUB's Academic Calendar.
  • Not red: a red band reads as a campus alert. And not for the result of something someone just did: that's a flash message. For an aside within a section, such as a tip or a warning, use a callout.

content wide, darkgrey invert, gold, blue invert

Link Bank

Red columns of links at the bottom of a page, to the site's main pages and its neighbors, as on the HUB. It's a grid with nav, and each column a nav-list; darkgrey invert works too, as on the ID+ pages. Keep it to links: a way into an app goes in the page, not here. A column can start with an <h1>, which shows as a small bold label.
