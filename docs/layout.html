Page Layout

The CMU template's own header, navigation, breadcrumbs, and footer, as on this page. Cascade builds them for you; an app writes them itself, using this markup.

Cascade Sites

Cascade writes all of this from the site's folders and settings, so on a Cascade site you only write what goes inside the content area. This page is for apps, and for knowing what the parts are called.

Try the Header

Change this site's own header to see the options, then open the menu to see what moves there. Your choices stay as you browse the rest of these pages, until you reset them or close the tab.

Acting as Another User
  • The roof, the template's name for the strip across the top with the wordmark: red invert for red, darkgrey invert for dark gray (as on Human Resources' pages), or nothing for white. Cascade sites set it in the setup block.
  • A logo sits right of the site's name, as on the Core4 Student Dashboard. On phones the template moves it below the name. See Site Name.
  • Search sits left of the menu button; see Search.
  • Signed out, a Sign In link sits in the roof and at the top of the menu. Signed in, your name sits in the roof and opens the menu, which starts with your name and Sign Out. On phones only the menu has them. See Sign In and Sign Out.
  • Acting as another user frames the whole window; see Acting as Another User.

The Whole Page

Every part has a fixed place. The navigation goes in the footer: the template's script copies it into the header and the menu, and without the script it stays a plain list in the footer.

<!DOCTYPE html>
<html lang="en">
<head>
  <meta charset="utf-8">
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0">
  <title>Page - Site Name - Carnegie Mellon University</title>
  <link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css?family=Open+Sans:400,400italic,300italic,300,700,700italic" rel="stylesheet">
  <link href="https://www.cmu.edu/common/standard-v6/css/main.6.7.min.css" media="screen" rel="stylesheet">
  <link href="https://www.cmu.edu/common/standard-v6/css/print.min.css" media="print" rel="stylesheet">
  <link href="https://www.cmu.edu/favicon.ico" rel="icon">
  <link href="https://www.cmu.edu/favicon-152.png" rel="apple-touch-icon-precomposed">
  <meta content="#9f0000" name="msapplication-TileColor">
  <meta content="https://www.cmu.edu/favicon-144.png" name="msapplication-TileImage">
  <!-- the kit, then your own stylesheet -->
</head>
<body class="no-js">
<div id="container">
  <header>
    <div class="roof red invert">
      <div>
        <a class="wordmark" href="https://www.cmu.edu/">Carnegie Mellon University</a>
        <button aria-label="toggle menu" aria-pressed="false" class="menu-btn" href="#nav"><b>&#8212;</b><b>&#8212;</b><b>&#8212;</b></button>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div id="sitename"> <!-- Site Name, below --> </div>
  </header>

  <main id="content">
    <div class="breadcrumbs"> <!-- Breadcrumbs, below --> </div>
    <div class="content">
      <div>
        <h1>Page Title</h1>
        <!-- the page -->
      </div>
    </div>
  </main>

  <footer class="grid column3 invert">
    <div>
      <p>Your Site or App<br>Carnegie Mellon University<br>5000 Forbes Avenue<br>Pittsburgh, PA 15213</p>
      <ul><li><a href="https://www.cmu.edu/legal/">Legal Info</a></li><li><a href="https://www.cmu.edu/">www.cmu.edu</a></li></ul>
      <ul><li>&copy; 2026 Carnegie Mellon University</li></ul>
    </div>
    <div></div>
    <div>
      <nav id="nav"> <!-- Navigation, below --> </nav>
    </div>
  </footer>
</div>
<!-- Scripts, below -->
</body>
</html>
  • Keep no-js on <body>. The template's script removes it, and the template styles the page differently until then.
  • The roof (div.roof) is the strip across the top with the wordmark. Add red invert for a red roof, as here, or darkgrey invert for dark gray; without either it's white.
  • The favicon is CMU's own, from www.cmu.edu, as on every CMS site: the four lines after the stylesheets.
  • The footer starts with the site or app name, the same as in the header, on the line above Carnegie Mellon University's address.
  • Add cmu-app to <body> in an app (see App Components).

Site Name

The site's name links to its home page. Under it, an optional <h2> names who runs it, such as a department, as a link to their site or as plain text.

<div id="sitename">
  <span class="sitetitle"><a href="/">Office Hours Queue</a></span>
  <h2><a href="https://www.cmu.edu/tli-office/">Teach, Learn, Innovate</a></h2>
</div>

For a logo, wrap the name and the <h2> in a <div> and put the logo, as a linked image, after it, as the Core4 Student Dashboard does with the Core Competencies Initiative's logo (try it on this page). The template puts the logo on the right, up to 150px tall, and below the name on phones. Use only an official logo for your unit from University Communications & Marketing. Leave no space between the </div> and the <a>: the two share the width exactly, so a space pushes the logo onto its own line. On phones the template moves the logo below the name and subtitle, at up to half the screen's width, so keep the image no larger than you want it shown there. See a Sample Page With a Logo

<div id="sitename">
  <div>
    <span class="sitetitle"><a href="/">Office Hours Queue</a></span>
    <h2><a href="https://www.cmu.edu/tli-office/">Teach, Learn, Innovate</a></h2>
  </div><a href="https://www.cmu.edu/tli-office/"><img src="/images/logo.png" alt="Teach, Learn, Innovate"></a>
</div>

Search

The template's search box goes in the roof, after the menu button. It searches cmu.edu, with a "Search this site only" box that appears when someone clicks in it; set that box's value to your Cascade site's ID. The template also puts a copy at the top of the menu, and on phones shows it as a row under the wordmark. See It on the Sample Page

<form action="https://search.cmu.edu" class="search search-form" method="get" name="customForm">
  <input name="form_id" type="hidden" value="basic-form">
  <input name="site" type="hidden" value="">
  <input name="ie" type="hidden" value="UTF-8">
  <label><span>Search</span> <input aria-label="Search" name="q" placeholder="Search"></label>
  <button title="Submit" type="submit">Search</button>
  <label class="searchSite"><span>Search this site only</span> <input checked name="site" type="checkbox" value="your-site-id"></label>
</form>
  • Cascade sites get it from the site's settings; you don't write it.
  • Apps usually leave it out. It searches cmu.edu, not your app, so it competes with the app's own search. Put the app's search on the page where its results appear, like the Syllabus Registry's Browse page.
  • With a Sign In link or your name too, put it after the form. The kit lines them up as search, your name, then the menu button, keeping your name beside the menu it opens.

Navigation

One list, nav#nav, in the footer's third column. The template's script turns it into two things:

  • Header tabs under the site name, from 768px up: the first ten top-level items, without their sub-lists. The one you're on is red and underlined.
  • The menu that slides out from the menu button, with every item and sub-list. Phones use only the menu.

Nest pages the way the site is organized: the top level holds a few sections, and each section's pages go in a sub-list under it, as deep as the site needs. The tabs then stay short, and the menu shows the whole site with the current section open. The home page isn't in the list: the site name links to it.

<nav id="nav">
  <ul>
    <li><a href="/queues">Queues</a></li>
    <li><a href="/my-courses">My Courses</a></li>
    <li>
      <a href="/help">Help</a>
      <ul>
        <li><a href="/help/students">For Students</a></li>
        <li>
          <a href="/help/staff">For Course Staff</a>
          <ul>
            <li class="active"><a href="/help/staff/open" aria-current="page">Open Office Hours</a></li>
            <li><a href="/help/staff/canvas">Link It From Canvas</a></li>
          </ul>
        </li>
      </ul>
    </li>
    <li class="secondary"><a class="external" href="https://www.cmu.edu/tli-office/">Teach, Learn, Innovate</a></li>
    <li class="secondary"><a href="/logout">Sign Out</a></li>
  </ul>
</nav>
MarkWhat it does
li.activeThe page you're on, red in the menu. Put it on the current page's own item, however deep; the template marks the items above it, so its section's tab turns red too and its sub-lists open. Add aria-current="page" to its link for screen readers.
A sub-list (ul in an li)A section's pages, shown only in the menu, folded under an arrow. Nest as deep as you need. The template opens the ones holding the active item.
A nested active itemOn a computer screen, when the current page is below the top level or has pages of its own, the menu starts open beside the page, so people can see where they are. Top-level pages with nothing under them start with it closed. Tablets and phones always start closed.
li.secondaryKept out of the tabs; in the menu, shown in lighter type. For links such as Contact Us or Sign Out.
a.externalAdds the template's leave-site icon to a link to another site.

This site's own menu is an example: Components is a section, with its three pages under it. Keep labels short and in Title Case, and keep to about six top-level items so the tabs fit on one row. To show the signed-in person beside the menu button, see Sign In and Sign Out.

Breadcrumbs

The trail from the site's home to the page, first inside #content. Each step before the current page is a link; the current page is plain text at the end. Leave them off the home page.

<div class="breadcrumbs">
  <a href="/">Office Hours Queue</a>&nbsp;&rsaquo;&nbsp;<a href="/help">Help</a>&nbsp;&rsaquo;&nbsp;For Course Staff
</div>

Separate the steps with &nbsp;&rsaquo;&nbsp; ( › ), as Cascade does, so a step never starts a new line with the arrow.

Content and Sidebar

.content lays out its direct children as columns. Put the page in one <div> inside it, and for a sidebar, add a second div.sidebar beside it; on phones the sidebar drops below.

<div class="content">
  <div>
    <h1>Page Title</h1>
    <p>The page.</p>
  </div><div class="sidebar">
    <div>
      <h2>Need Help?</h2>
      <p>Boxes in the sidebar.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
  • Leave no space between the page's </div> and <div class="sidebar">, as above. The two columns fill the width exactly, so even a line break between them pushes the sidebar below the page. It's the same trap as a logo in the header.
  • Without wide, the page takes about 60% of the width, leaving room for the sidebar. Add wide to give it the full width when there's no sidebar.
  • Several .content blocks can follow one another, for a page in bands.

Scripts

At the end of <body>, from the template, in this order. Without them the page still works, with the navigation as a list in the footer.

<script src="https://www.cmu.edu/common/standard-v6/js/list.min.js"></script>
<script src="https://www.cmu.edu/common/standard-v6/js/jquery-1.10.1.min.js"></script>
<script src="https://www.cmu.edu/common/standard-v6/js/main.6.7.min.js"></script>

Besides the navigation, the script removes style="" from spans, and opens and closes the template's .collapsed headings.

Navigation or Tabs?

  • Pages of the site go in the navigation: Browse, My Courses, Help.
  • Views of one page go in tabs (cmu-tabs) at the top of the content: semesters, or a list's sections.
  • Where you are goes in the breadcrumbs, when a page sits below another.
