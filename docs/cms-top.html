Page Tops

What goes at the top of a page: an eyebrow, a lead and buttons, and a hero with screenshots.

Eyebrow, Lead, and Call-to-Action Row

An eyebrow is an optional short red line right above a heading. Use it on a page or card meant for one group ("For Students," as on path cards), or to flag something new ("New for Spring 2027"). Leave it off a page for everyone, such as an app's home page, and never repeat the site's name in it: that's already the big title at the top of every page. A lead is the larger first paragraph under a page title. The call-to-action row lines up a button, a link, and an optional note.

Hero

The top of a home page: copy on the left, art on the right. It becomes one column below 820px. Lead with a tagline that says what the app does, then one main button. The tagline is the page's <h1>, and so is each section's heading after it (see Page Sections). The art can be:

  • One screenshot, drawn in a browser frame.
  • Two or three screenshots, overlapping, with cmu-hero-art--stack. They're cropped to the same shape from the top, and the last one is in front, so put the main one last.
  • A photo or a tartan with no frame, with cmu-hero-art--plain. Use the tartan files from brand.cmu.edu as they come: the brand rules don't allow redrawing or recoloring the tartan.

cmu-hero, cmu-hero-art, cmu-hero-art--stack, cmu-hero-art--plain
